3.2 Key Takeaway: Why ElasticNet Dominates

With N=42, ElasticNet outperforms both XGBoost and the stacked ensemble on every target. This is expected: tree-based models require substantially more observations to discover non-linear interaction effects without overfitting. The stacked meta-learner has only 41 training points per outer fold, insufficient to learn optimal base-learner weights. At this sample size, the bias–variance tradeoff strongly favours the simpler linear model.

PNL is genuinely unpredictable from structural county-level features. The bootstrap CI crossing zero (−0.27, +0.32) confirms this is not a data quality issue — PNL votes appear driven by local factors (candidate incumbency, mayoral networks, urban/rural split of specific cities) that are invisible at the NUTS 3 aggregation level. This is an informative null finding.

Practical implication: For a 2025 parliamentary scenario, AUR, PSD, SOS, and UDMR can be predicted with meaningful confidence intervals. PNL and POT require county-specific qualitative input (e.g., incumbency effects, candidate quality) that structural data cannot capture.

4. Key Findings

4.1 Georgescu → Simion Vote Transfer (r = 0.884, p < 0.0001)

The single most striking finding in the dataset. Across all 42 counties, the correlation between Georgescu's annulled November 2024 first-round share and Simion's May 2025 first-round share is 0.884. This implies:

  • The anti-establishment nationalist vote is geographically entrenched, not personality-driven. Annulling Georgescu did not disrupt the underlying geographic coalition.
  • The nationalist_consolidation_ratio (mean = 0.345) shows Georgescu captured ~35% of the combined Georgescu+Simion total. Harghita (0.281) and Mehedinți (0.272) are outliers on the low end — both have structural explanations (Harghita = UDMR dominance suppressing nationalist vote; Mehedinți = weak Georgescu penetration despite high grievance).
  • Simion's absolute shares (mean = 40.1%, range 9.0%–51.2%) substantially exceed Georgescu's (mean = 21.3%), consistent with vote consolidation from AUR's 2024 parliamentary base.
See figures/georgescu_simion_transfer.png for the scatter plot with county labels.

4.2 The Anti-Establishment Geographic Topology

Top 5 AUR counties in 2024 parliamentary: Gorj (27.1%), Suceava (26.1%), Ialomița (24.6%), Arad (23.7%), Neamț (23.4%).

These span different regions (Oltenia, Moldova, Muntenia, Transylvania) and different economic profiles. What they share is high Simion vote share and moderate-to-high grievance indices. The AUR coalition is not regionally monolithic — it is socially distributed across multiple "profiles" of discontent.

4.3 USR Urban Core

Top USR counties: București (23.2%), Timiș (20.1%), Brașov (18.3%), Ilfov (17.8%), Sibiu (16.7%). These are the highest-modernity, highest-migration-inflow counties. net_migration_rate is USR's #1 SHAP feature — counties attracting internal migrants vote more reform-oriented.

4.4 Turnout Crisis in Moldova

Lowest turnout counties: Vaslui (29.1%), Botoșani (36.2%), Iași (36.3%), Bacău (36.6%), Ialomița (37.0%). These are among the highest-emigration counties in Moldova. The SHAP evidence confirms: emigrants_permanent_rate is the strongest predictor of low turnout, suggesting a direct mechanism (registered voters who have emigrated but remain on rolls).

4.5 Spatial Autocorrelation: None Found

Moran's I tests on OLS residuals for AUR (I = −0.045, p = 0.428), PSD (I = −0.066, p = 0.301), and ANTI_ESTABLISHMENT (I = −0.125, p = 0.133) are all non-significant. This means once structural features are controlled for, there is no residual geographic clustering. The apparent regional patterns in raw vote shares (e.g., Moldova/Oltenia AUR concentration) are explained by the structural variables, not by spatial spillover effects.

6. Toward Voter Profiles

The current model treats each county as an independent data point and predicts vote shares from structural features. An alternative and complementary framing is to identify latent voter profiles — clusters of counties with similar socio-economic and demographic structures that also share similar voting patterns. This offers several advantages:

  • Interpretability: "This county belongs to the grievance-rural profile" is more communicable than a linear combination of 20 coefficients.
  • Prediction generalisation: Profiles can be extrapolated to new scenarios (e.g., "if county X shifts from Profile 2 to Profile 3, what happens to AUR?").
  • Avoiding overfitting: Clustering reduces the problem dimensionality before prediction, acting as a regularisation step.

7. Brainstorm: Ideas for Next Iteration

7.1 Voter Profile Clustering (High Priority)

What: k-means / GMM on feature space → cluster-conditional vote share prediction

Run unsupervised clustering (k=4 to 7, test with silhouette + elbow) on a curated set of structural features only (no vote shares). Then analyse within-cluster vote distributions. A two-stage pipeline:

  1. Stage 1: Cluster counties into voter profiles using structural features (GDP/cap, grievance, urban share, emigration rate, ethnic frags).
  2. Stage 2: Within each cluster, fit a lighter linear model on vote shares. Cluster membership is the dominant variable; within-cluster residuals reveal idiosyncratic county effects.

Why this helps: If the 42 counties fall into, say, 5 coherent profiles, and those profiles explain 60% of AUR variance, the remaining 40% is county-level "noise" we can model separately. This also gives a natural language for the report: "In Grievance-Rural counties, AUR averages 22%; in Urban-Cosmopolitan counties, 7%."

Open question for you: Should clusters be defined purely on structural/demographic features (no election data), or should past election data be allowed in the clustering? Pure structural clustering makes out-of-sample generalisation cleaner but may miss politically-relevant groupings.

7.2 Additional Data Angles

Diaspora Vote Returns

The roaep.ro data likely includes votes cast abroad by county of registration. These are currently excluded. A diaspora share variable would capture the gap between registered and resident voters — directly relevant to the turnout crisis finding.

Feasibility: Check if roaep.ro has "circumscriptia diaspora" rows; could be ingested in the current pipeline.

Mayor/Prefect Partisan Affiliation

Local administrative control (PSD mayor, PNL mayor) strongly influences parliamentary vote shares through mobilisation and patronage networks. This would explain PNL's unpredictability — it's a party where who runs locally matters more than structural factors.

Feasibility: AEP (Autoritatea Electorala Permanenta) publishes local election results by county. Could add as a categorical or share variable.

2020 Parliamentary Data (Pending)

Once downloaded, enables: swing variables (2020→2024 party-level change), lagged vote shares as predictors, and trend analysis. The anti-establishment swing is currently blank; filling it would add the most explanatory variable we're missing.

Caution: 2020 was COVID election with artificially low turnout. Use as predictor, not baseline for "normal" behaviour.

Senate vs. Chamber Split

Voters can split tickets between Senate and Camera Deputatilor. The divergence between the two reveals preference intensity and strategic voting behaviour. Counties with large Senate/Camera splits are interesting anomalies for profile analysis.

7.3 Modelling Enhancements

Dirichlet Regression (R)

Vote shares for all parties within a county must sum to 1 — they are compositional data. Separate OLS on each party ignores this constraint. Dirichlet regression models all parties jointly, respects the simplex constraint, and produces coherent joint predictions. Best implemented in R via the DirichletReg package. Suitable for the final report methodology section.

Profile-Conditional Prediction Intervals

Rather than a single R² for all 42 counties, report prediction intervals conditioned on cluster membership. "For counties in the Grievance-Rural cluster, AUR prediction interval is [18%, 27%]." This is more honest and more actionable for scenario analysis.

Counterfactual Scenario Engine

Given the voter profile clusters, build a scenario tool: "What if Gorj's GDP/cap increased by 20%?" → shift the feature vector, re-predict. This requires fixing the rest of the feature vector and varying one dimension — exactly what PDPs do, but applied to specific counties rather than the average.

Cross-Election Validation

The 2025 Presidential R2 (Dan vs. Simion) is already in the data as a validation target. Build a model that predicts Dan vs. Simion split from the same structural features and evaluate against observed. This is a strong out-of-sample test because it uses a completely different election.

7.4 Overfitting Risk Management

Current risk level: Moderate. With 42 counties and ~20 predictors in OLS, and ~35 predictors in EN/XGBoost, the models are operating at the boundary of what's defensible. The mitigations already in place (LOOCV, bootstrap CIs, LRO-CV, VIF monitoring) are appropriate. Adding more features without increasing N is counterproductive. The voter profile clustering approach is specifically designed to reduce effective dimensionality rather than increase it.

The most likely forms of overfitting in this dataset:

  • Overfitting to the 2024 anti-establishment wave: The georgescu/simion/ep_2024 variables are collinear proxies for the same underlying signal. The model may be "memorising" this wave rather than learning the structural drivers. Testing on 2020 data will reveal this.
  • Small-cluster artefacts: Budapest (UDMR) and Ilfov are genuine outliers. LOOCV handles them individually, but any model that explains these two perfectly is likely overfitting.
  • Interaction term proliferation: The 8 engineered features (rural_elderly, etc.) are all correlated with their component variables. EN regularises this, but in the report these should be presented as exploratory rather than confirmed predictors.

8. Recommended Next Steps

  1. Voter profile clustering — k-means on structural features (k=4–7), silhouette selection, named profiles with vote share distributions. This is the highest-value next step.
  2. Ingest 2020 parliamentary data — unlocks swing variables and lagged predictors. Adds the most missing explanatory power.
  3. Ingest diaspora vote returns — directly testable if roaep.ro has abroad rows.
  4. Cross-election validation — predict Dan/Simion 2025 R2 split from structural features; compare to observed.
  5. Dirichlet regression in R Markdown — for the final report methodology section; models the full party vote share vector jointly.
  6. Mayor/prefect partisanship variable — would explain PNL's unpredictability (data available from AEP local election results).
  7. Scenario analysis tool — county-level counterfactual predictions using the fitted EN model.
